# [c2] Quick Rescue
GUIDETABLE|What you see|Most likely cause|What to do first||Seedling wilts after planting|Transplant shock, hot sun, dry root ball|Shade 1 to 2 days, water gently, and avoid fertilizer until recovery.||Leaves yellow while soil is wet|Overwatering, poor drainage, root stress|Reduce watering, improve drainage, and keep pots off wet ground.||Tiny white insects fly up|Whiteflies|Spray under leaves, use yellow sticky traps, repeat every 3 to 5 days.||Flowers dropping|Heat stress, water stress, low pollination, excess nitrogen|Water evenly, mulch, reduce heavy nitrogen, and avoid severe drying.||Dark sunken spot on fruit bottom|Blossom-end rot from uneven water|Keep watering even, mulch, and avoid root damage.||Plant suddenly collapses|Root rot, bacterial wilt, or severe root damage|Remove badly affected plant and improve drainage.

# [c2] Quick Numbers
GUIDEGRID|Crop::Sweet pepper / bell pepper||Botanical name::Capsicum annuum||Sunlight::6 to 10 hours daily||Soil::Rich, loose, well-drained soil||Water::Even moisture; do not let roots sit soggy||Transplant timing::Plant out after hardening off and when seedlings are strong||Spacing::18 to 24 inches apart for most home gardens||Harvest::Pick green or allow fruit to mature to yellow, orange, red, or variety color

# [c2] Care Basics
GUIDETABLE|Care area|Guide||Sun|Give strong light. In very hot conditions, protect from harsh afternoon stress.||Soil|Use rich soil with compost and reliable drainage. Avoid compacted or waterlogged areas.||Water|Keep moisture steady. Uneven watering is a major cause of blossom-end rot.||Feeding|Start gentle feeding after transplant recovery. Use less nitrogen once flowering begins.||Mulch|Mulch helps keep moisture even and keeps fruit cleaner.||Support|Stake plants if fruit load gets heavy or wind is strong.

# [c2] Pests
# [c2] Diseases
GUIDETABLE|Problem|Signs|Action||Aphids|Sticky curled new growth|Spray undersides early and repeat checks.||Whiteflies|White insects fly up when plant is disturbed|Spray undersides, use sticky traps, manage weeds.||Mites|Speckled leaves, fine webbing|Rinse undersides and reduce drought stress.||Caterpillars|Chewed leaves or fruit holes|Check early morning and handpick.||Bacterial/fungal spots|Brown or black leaf spots|Remove affected leaves, improve airflow, avoid wet leaves.||Root rot|Wilting with wet soil|Improve drainage and avoid soggy soil.
