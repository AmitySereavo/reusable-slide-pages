## [c3] For Piper nigrum vines: transplanting, support, shade, watering, feeding, pest and disease care, and harvest expectations.
BR
GUIDEGRID|Crop::Black pepper / peppercorn vine||Botanical name::Piper nigrum||Growth habit::Perennial climbing vine with aerial roots||Customer plant type::Potted/rooted vine plant||Main need::Strong support plus moist, well-drained soil plus filtered light
BR
[c3] Important: this guide is for the black pepper plant that produces peppercorns. It is not Scotch bonnet, sweet pepper, or hot pepper.

# [c2] Quick Rescue
BR
GUIDETABLE|What you see|Most likely cause|What to do first||New plant wilts after planting|Transplant shock, dry root ball, hot sun|Move to bright shade for 2 to 3 days, water gently, keep soil evenly moist but not soggy.||Leaves pale or yellow, slow growth|Low fertility, water stress, too much sun, poor roots|Check drainage, add compost, feed lightly, keep in filtered light.||Leaf tips or edges brown|Sun scorch, dry soil, fertilizer burn, salt buildup|Give afternoon shade, water deeply, avoid strong fertilizer for 10 to 14 days.||Vine not climbing|No support or no training|Tie loosely to a pole, trellis, or living support every 8 to 12 inches of new growth.||Sticky leaves or cottony insects|Mealybugs, scale insects, aphids|Wipe or prune bad spots; spray mild soap/neem carefully every 5 to 7 days.||Stem base darkens and vine collapses|Foot rot / quick wilt risk|Improve drainage immediately; remove badly diseased vines; avoid water sitting at the base.||Black or brown spots on leaves|Anthracnose / leaf spot disease|Remove affected leaves, avoid overhead watering, increase spacing and airflow.

# [c2] Quick Numbers
BR
GUIDEGRID|Best climate::Humid tropical, about 23 to 32 C / 73 to 90 F||Cold stress risk::Below about 15 C / 59 F||Sunlight::Filtered light / partial shade||Shade target::About 25 to 50 percent shade||Field spacing::2 to 3 m apart; common pure crop spacing 3 m x 3 m||Home spacing::At least 6 to 10 ft from another large vine/support||Container size::Minimum 5 gallons; 10 to 15 gallons better long term||Support height::Start with 4 to 6 ft support||Root settling::14 to 30 days after transplant||Visible new growth::2 to 6 weeks after transplant||First flowering::Usually 2 to 3 years under good conditions||First meaningful harvest::Usually 3 to 4 years after planting

# [c2] Transplanting
## [c3] Plant it where it can climb and where water will not sit around the roots.
BR
GUIDETABLE|Before transplanting|Do this||Water|Water the potted plant 1 to 2 hours before transplanting.||Timing|Choose early morning before 8:30 a.m. or late afternoon after 4:30 p.m.||Support|Prepare pole, trellis, fence, or living support tree before planting.||Root ball|Keep the root ball together; do not shake soil off the roots.||Acclimation|If the plant was under shade, harden it off for 5 to 7 days.
BR
GUIDETABLE|Step|What to do||1|Dig a hole wider than the root ball; in poor soil prepare about 18 to 20 inches wide and deep.||2|Mix compost or well-rotted organic matter into the backfill soil.||3|Plant at the same depth it was growing in the pot.||4|Press soil gently around roots; do not compact hard.||5|Shape soil so water drains outward and does not collect at the stem base.||6|Water immediately after planting.||7|Tie the vine loosely to support; do not strangle the stem.||8|Shade for 2 to 3 days if the sun is hot.
BR
[c3] Do not bury the stem deeply and do not let mulch touch the stem base.

# [c2] Support, Spacing, and Layout
BR
GUIDETABLE|Growing setup|Recommended layout||Home garden single plant|One strong 4 to 6 ft pole, trellis, fence section, or living support. Keep base well drained.||Small farmer / field|2 to 3 m spacing between supports; 3 m x 3 m is common commercial layout.||Container plant|Use a 5 to 15 gallon pot with drainage and a firm pole/trellis tied into the pot.||Living support tree|Use a support that can be pruned to give filtered light and airflow. Avoid dense shade.||Wall/fence growing|Keep vine off hot metal surfaces and avoid roof water running onto the root zone.
BR
GUIDEGRID|Training::Tie new growth every 8 to 12 inches using soft ties||Tie fit::Keep ties loose enough for stem expansion||Direction::Guide the vine upward; do not let it crawl along wet soil||Pruning::Remove dead, diseased, or tangled growth||Base care::Keep the lower base area clean and airy

# [c2] Sunlight, Shade, Water, and Soil
BR
GUIDETABLE|Need|Target||Light|Bright filtered light or partial shade; morning sun is safer than harsh afternoon sun.||Shade|About 25 to 50 percent in hot lowland areas.||Soil|Loose, fertile, well-drained soil high in organic matter.||Drainage|Water must drain away from the base; raised mound or bed is better in wet spots.||Water|Keep evenly moist. Do not let the pot dry completely; do not keep it waterlogged.||Mulch|2 to 3 inches organic mulch, kept 2 to 4 inches away from the stem.||pH|Slightly acidic to near-neutral, about 5.5 to 7.0.
BR
[c3] Black pepper likes moisture, but foot rot likes stagnant wet soil. Moist and drained is good; soggy and airless is dangerous.

# [c2] Feeding and Soil Care
BR
GUIDETABLE|Stage|Timing|Feeding direction||At transplant|Day 0|Compost or well-rotted organic matter mixed into planting area. Avoid strong fertilizer directly on roots.||Settling period|First 14 to 30 days|Water consistently. Do not push hard fertilizer while roots recover.||Active growth|After new growth starts|Light balanced feeding every 4 to 6 weeks in ground, or every 3 to 4 weeks in containers.||Rainy season|Wet periods|Avoid overfeeding nitrogen; focus on drainage, mulch, disease prevention.||Mature bearing vine|After 2 to 3 years|Use compost plus balanced plant food. Add potassium support during flowering/spike development.
BR
[c3] Do not overfeed a stressed, wilted, or newly transplanted plant.

# [c2] Common Pests
BR
GUIDETABLE|Pest|Signs|Action||Pollu beetle / flea beetle|Damage to tender leaves, spikes, berries; berries may become hollow or drop|Prune damaged parts, regulate shade, improve airflow, inspect young spikes early.||Scale insects|Brown or white bumps on stems or undersides; sticky residue; weak growth|Wipe or prune bad branches; spray mild oil/neem carefully; repeat in 7 to 10 days.||Mealybugs|White cottony clusters at nodes, roots, or leaf joints; ants may appear|Remove clusters, control ants, isolate badly infested potted plants.||Leaf gall thrips|Raised/gall-like leaf marks; distorted young leaves|Remove badly affected leaves, treat undersides, reduce overcrowding.||Mites|Fine speckling, dull leaves, sometimes webbing; worse in dry heat|Rinse undersides, keep plant evenly watered, treat every 5 to 7 days.||Nematodes|Weak roots, slow decline, yellowing, poor fertilizer response|Use healthy plants, add organic matter, avoid moving infected soil.

# [c2] Common Diseases
BR
GUIDETABLE|Disease / problem|Signs|Action||Foot rot / quick wilt|Base darkening, sudden wilt, leaf drop, vine collapse|Improve drainage, keep base dry, remove badly infected vines.||Slow wilt / decline|Gradual yellowing, leaf shedding, poor roots, weak growth|Add organic matter, reduce root stress, avoid waterlogging, improve soil health.||Anthracnose / pollu disease|Brown or black leaf spots, dieback, damaged berries or spikes|Remove infected leaves/spikes, improve airflow, avoid overhead watering.||Charcoal rot / stem disease|Dark dry lesions, weak stem, decline under stress|Avoid drought stress, mulch, remove affected tissue.||Root rot in pots|Wet soil smell, brown soft roots, wilting though soil is wet|Repot into fresh draining mix, trim dead roots, use pot with drainage.||Viral symptoms|Mosaic patterns, distorted leaves, stunting|Control sucking insects, prune or remove if severe, wash tools and hands.
BR
[c3] Strong protection starts with clean planting material, drainage, mulch kept away from the stem, airflow, and early removal of diseased parts.

# [c2] Leaf Color and Plant Problem Guide
BR
GUIDETABLE|Leaf look|Possible cause|What to do||Deep green, firm leaves|Healthy growth|Continue steady water, filtered light, and support training.||Light green new leaves|Normal if it darkens in 7 to 14 days|Watch; feed lightly only if whole plant stays pale.||Pale/yellow all over|Low nutrients, root stress, water issue, too much sun|Check roots and drainage first, then feed lightly.||Brown crispy patches|Sunburn, dry root ball, fertilizer burn|Move to filtered light, water deeply, avoid strong fertilizer.||Black/brown wet spots|Leaf spot or rot|Remove affected leaves, reduce wet leaves, improve airflow.||Curled/distorted new leaves|Thrips, mites, sucking pests, chemical drift|Inspect undersides and new shoots; remove damaged growth.||Wilting midday only|Heat/sun stress or dry soil|Shade afternoon, mulch, check soil moisture.||Wilting morning and evening|Root problem or disease|Check drainage and roots; isolate if severe.

# [c2] Growth Timeline and Harvest Expectations
BR
GUIDETABLE|Stage|Expected time||After transplant: settling|14 to 30 days.||Visible active climbing growth|2 to 6 weeks after transplant.||Needs strong support|Immediately; train from the first week.||First flowering/spikes|Usually 2 to 3 years after planting, depending on age and care.||First small harvest|Often from year 3 under good conditions.||Fuller production|Often year 4 onward.||Harvest season|Depends on rainfall and flowering cycle; harvest spikes when berries are mature but not fully dry.||Processing black pepper|Harvest mature green/yellowing spikes, blanch optional, dry until peppercorns are hard and black.||Processing white pepper|Fully ripe red berries are soaked/processed to remove outer skin, then dried.
BR
[c3] Black pepper is a long-term spice vine. It is valuable, but it is not a quick crop like lettuce, tomato, or Scotch bonnet pepper.

# [c2] Home Garden and Small Farmer Notes
BR
GUIDETABLE|Grower type|Best approach||Home garden customers buying 1 to 3 plants|Plant near a strong pole, trellis, fence, or living support. Use a 5 to 15 gallon container if not planting in ground. Give bright filtered light. Keep soil moist but never waterlogged. Expect vine growth first; harvest comes later.||Small farmers planting several vines|Plan support before buying many plants. Use 2 to 3 m spacing; 3 m x 3 m is common. Avoid wet low spots. Plant on mounds or raised beds where water collects. Mulch well but keep mulch away from stem. Scout weekly for pests and wilt problems.
BR
GUIDEGRID|Clean hands::Wash before handling vines, especially after touching diseased plants||Tools::Clean pruners before moving plant to plant||Smoking::Do not smoke over plants or rest cigarette butts in pots or beds||Soil movement::Do not move soil from a sick vine to a healthy vine

# [c2] Weekly Care Checklist
BR
GUIDETABLE|How often|What to check||Daily for first 14 days|Soil moisture, wilting, sunburn, loose ties, and drainage around the stem.||Every 3 to 5 days|Look under leaves and around nodes for mealybugs, scale, thrips, mites, ants, and sticky residue.||Every 7 days|Train new growth to support, remove dead leaves, check mulch, inspect for leaf spots.||Every 3 to 4 weeks|Feed lightly if active growth is visible; inspect roots and pot drainage for container plants.||Every 2 to 3 months|Add compost or mulch; prune support plant or surrounding growth for filtered light and airflow.
BR

Send clear photos of the full plant, stem base, underside of leaves, soil or pot, support setup, and any insects or spots you see.
