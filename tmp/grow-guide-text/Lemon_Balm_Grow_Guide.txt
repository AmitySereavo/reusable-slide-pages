Hardening Off and Transplanting from Trays
Seedlings from trays should not be moved straight from shade or nursery conditions into harsh sun. Harden
them off first so they adjust to wind, brighter light, and outdoor moisture changes.
5-7 Day Hardening-Off Schedule
Day
Exposure
Day 1-2
Bright shade for 2-3 hours; keep tray moist
Day 3-4
Morning sun for 2-4 hours, then bright shade
Day 5-6
Morning sun plus normal outdoor airflow; avoid midday heat
Day 7
Ready to transplant, preferably late evening
Transplanting Steps
 Water tray 1-2 hours before planting so the root ball stays together.
 Prepare the hole or container before removing seedlings from the tray.
 Gently push the seedling out from the bottom of the cell; do not pull by the stem.
 Plant at the same depth as the tray soil line; do not bury the crown too deeply.
 Press soil gently around the roots and water immediately.
 Give light shade for 2-3 days in hot weather, then move gradually into morning sun.
 Check the plant morning and evening for the first 3-5 days.

Container Advice
 Use pots with drainage holes. Lemon balm can rot in soil that stays wet.
 Containers help control spread because lemon balm can self-seed and spread by rhizomes.
 For a customer buying 2-6 plants, containers or a raised herb bed are easiest to manage.
 For small farmers, keep rows wide enough for easy cutting, weeding, and airflow.

Sunlight, Heat, and Water
Lemon balm grows in full sun to part shade. In hot Jamaican conditions, morning sun plus afternoon shade is
safer for soft, flavorful leaves.
Need
Target
Daily sunlight
4-6 hours morning sun in hot areas; 6+ hours if weather is mild
Afternoon shade
Recommended from about 12 p.m. to 3 p.m. in harsh heat
Water after transplant
Check daily for 7 days; soil should be moist, not soggy
Established plants
Water when top 1 inch of soil begins to dry
Container plants
May need water daily in hot, dry weather
Overwatering warning
Wet, clumping soil + yellow leaves = reduce watering and improve drainage
Good Watering Practice
 Water at soil level where possible.
 Avoid keeping leaves wet overnight; wet leaves increase disease risk.
 Mulch lightly with dry grass, dry leaves, or compost to hold moisture and reduce heat stress.
 Keep mulch 1-2 inches away from the stem base to reduce rot.

Soil and Feeding Guide
Lemon balm can grow in many soils, but the best customer result comes from moist, rich, well-drained soil.
Fertilize lightly because overfeeding can make fast, soft growth with weaker flavor.
Item
Guide
Best soil
Rich, humus-filled, moist, well-drained
pH target
6.0-7.5 practical target
At transplant
Mix compost into soil; avoid fresh manure touching roots
First feeding
10-14 days after transplant if plant is growing well
Regular feeding
Light compost or mild balanced feed every 4-6 weeks if harvesting often
After cutting harvest
Light feeding can encourage fresh leaf growth
Avoid
Heavy fertilizer, waterlogged soil, pots without drainage
Simple Container Mix
 50% potting mix or good topsoil
 30% compost
 20% coco coir, dry leaf mold, or other material that improves structure and drainage
 Do not use heavy soil that stays muddy in a pot.

Spray Safety
 Test any spray on a few leaves first and wait 24 hours.
 Spray early morning or late evening, not in hot sun.
 Use mild soap, not harsh detergent.
 Repeat treatments are usually needed because eggs and hidden insects can survive one spray.

Disease Prevention
 Space plants so leaves dry quickly after rain or watering.
 Water the soil, not the leaves, especially late in the day.
 Remove diseased leaves from the garden area; do not leave them under the plant.
 Use clean scissors or pruners when harvesting.
 Do not plant new lemon balm in the same disease-heavy soil without improving drainage and sanitation.

Pruning and Harvesting for Better Leaves
Regular harvesting keeps lemon balm bushy, reduces flowering, and helps keep the leaves tender and flavorful.
Action
Timing / Amount
First light harvest
21-30 days after transplant, only if plant is growing strongly
Regular harvest
Cut up to 1/3 of the plant about once per month
Best flavor timing
Harvest before flowers open
After flowering
Cut back hard to encourage a fresh flush of leaves
Established plants
2-3 larger cuttings can be made during a productive season
Drying bundles
Tie 5-7 sprigs and hang in cool shade with good airflow
How to Harvest
 Use clean scissors; do not bruise the leaves heavily.
 Cut above a leaf node so the plant can branch again.
 Harvest in the morning after dew dries, before strong heat.
 For tea, use fresh leaves or dry them in shade; direct sun reduces quality.
 Do not remove more than 1/3 of a young plant at one time.

Controlling Spread and Keeping Plants Neat
Lemon balm is in the mint family. It is easier to control than some mints, but it can still spread by seed and
rhizomes if left unmanaged.
Issue
Prevention
Self-seeding
Cut flower stalks before seeds mature
Plant spreading sideways
Grow in container or trim edges monthly
Bed getting crowded
Divide or thin plants every 2-3 years
Woody or tired plant
Cut back after flowering and feed lightly
Weak flavor
Harvest young growth; avoid overfertilizing
Small Farmer Quality Note
For customers growing lemon balm for tea or bundles, clean leaves are the product. Keep weeds down, harvest
before flowering, dry in shade, and avoid mixing weeds or diseased leaves into harvested material.

Small Farmer and Home Garden Notes
For Small Farmers
 Use 18-24 inch spacing for plants that will be cut repeatedly.
 Leave 24-36 inches between rows for harvesting and airflow.
 Plant in batches if you want continuous fresh bundles for customers.
 Harvest before flowering for best leaf quality and to reduce self-seeding.
 Dry leaves in shade with airflow; store only when fully dry.
For Home Garden Customers Buying 2-6 Plants
 Use one plant per 1-3 gallon pot, or space plants 18 inches apart in a bed.
 Place where the plant gets morning sun and some afternoon shade.
 Harvest small amounts often for tea, seasoning, drinks, or scent.
 Keep in a pot if you do not want it to spread into the garden.
 Send photos by WhatsApp if leaves yellow, rot, spot, or get pests.

Reference Notes Used for This Guide
This customer guide is written in ParaLife Trees language for seedlings sold in Jamaica. The practical ranges
are adapted from extension and horticulture references, then adjusted for tray seedlings, containers, small
gardens, and hot tropical conditions.
 Utah State University Extension: How to Grow Lemon Balm in Your Garden - spacing 18-24 inches, moist well-drained soil, sparing
fertilizer, insects/diseases, and monthly harvest guidance.
 University of Wisconsin-Madison Extension: Lemon Balm, Melissa officinalis - plant height, seedling transplant size, 16-18 inch spacing,
harvest/cutback, and pest/disease notes.
 UC Master Gardeners / UC ANR: Lemon Balm - plant 2 feet apart, full sun to part shade, avoid overwatering, aphids/whiteflies/mites, leaf
spot spacing/rotation.
 NC State Extension Gardener Plant Toolbox: Melissa officinalis - container growing, sun to part shade, self-seeding/rhizome spread, and
disease list.
 UF/IFAS Santa Rosa County Cooperative Extension: Lemon Balm - 24-36 inch height, 18-36 inch spread, 12-36 inch spacing, full sun to
part shade, container use, and no serious pests/diseases.
This guide is for plant care support and does not provide medical advice or promise health benefits from lemon balm.
