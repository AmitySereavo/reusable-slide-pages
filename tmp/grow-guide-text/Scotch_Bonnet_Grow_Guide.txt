Brown, black, or yellow leaf spots. Leaves

Plant wilts suddenly; may not recover

Remove badly affected plants. Avoid

Southern blight / stem
rot
White fungal growth near soil line; plant
collapses; stem base rots.
Remove plant and infected soil near stem.
Keep mulch away from stem. Improve airflow
and drainage.
Anthracnose fruit rot
Sunken spots on fruit; fruit may rot before
harvest.
Remove infected fruits. Avoid overhead
watering. Harvest regularly.
Tobacco/smoker warning: Pepper plants can be affected by tobacco mosaic-type viruses. Smokers and
workers who handle tobacco products should wash hands with soap and water before touching seedlings,
leaves, trays, pruning tools, or fruit. Use clean gloves for tray handling and transplanting.

4. Transplanting Seedlings From Trays
This is the most important section for customers buying seedlings. A healthy seedling can still suffer if it is moved from
tray to field or pot too quickly, planted too deep, or allowed to dry out during transplanting.
Seedling readiness numbers
Item
Target range
Age from sowing
5-8 weeks, depending on growing conditions
Height
12-15 cm / 5-6 inches is a good transplant size
Leaves
At least 4-6 true leaves; leaves should be green and not badly curled
Roots
Root ball should hold together, but should not be severely root-bound
Transplant time
Evening is best; early morning is second best
First week after transplant
Keep well-watered for 7 days while roots settle
Step-by-step transplanting
●
Water the tray 1-2 hours before removing seedlings.
●
Handle seedlings by the root ball or leaves, not by squeezing the stem.
●
Keep the root ball intact. Bare-root or damaged-root seedlings recover more slowly.
●
Plant at the same depth the seedling was growing in the tray or pot.
●
Press soil gently around the root ball so there are no large air pockets.
●
Water immediately after transplanting.
●
Check daily for the first 7 days. Replace dead or weak plants early if planting for production.
Do not let tray seedlings sit in direct hot sun after purchase while waiting to plant. Keep them watered, bright,
and protected until transplanting.

5. Hardening Off Before Full Sun
Hardening off means training seedlings to handle stronger sun, wind, and field conditions before they are planted
permanently.
7-day hardening plan
Day
Sun exposure
Care note
Day 1-2
2-3 hours morning sun
Keep seedlings watered; avoid midday sun.
Day 3-4
3-5 hours sun
Allow more airflow; do not let trays dry out.
Day 5-6
5-7 hours sun
Expose to near-final conditions; check for wilting.
Day 7
6-8 hours sun if plants stay firm
Ready to transplant in evening if roots and leaves look healthy.
If seedlings wilt badly during hardening, reduce sun exposure for 24 hours, water properly, and restart with
fewer hours of sun. Hardening should toughen the plant, not burn it.
For small farmers planting trays: schedule hardening so seedlings are field-ready before labour arrives. Avoid
buying/collecting seedlings and leaving them stressed in trays for several extra days.

6. Spacing for Small Farmers and Home Gardens
Use wider spacing for easier harvesting, better airflow, and lower disease pressure. Use closer spacing only when you
can manage pruning, airflow, irrigation, feeding, and pest checks properly.

12. Harvesting Guide
Harvest item
Specific guidance
Green harvest
Harvest when fruit reaches full size and is firm.
Ripe harvest
Harvest when fruit reaches mature variety color: yellow, orange, red,
chocolate/brown, or other type.
Harvest frequency
Every 5-10 days during active production.
Handling
Use scissors/pruner to avoid breaking branches. Wear gloves if harvesting many
peppers.
After harvest
Keep fruit shaded and cool. Sort damaged or diseased fruit away from clean fruit.
Harvesting regularly encourages continued production and helps remove damaged fruit before pests and diseases
spread.

13. Weekly Care Checklist
Frequency
Checklist
Every 1-2 days
Check soil moisture; look under leaves; check for whiteflies; check for wilting; inspect
flowers and young fruit.
Every 7 days
Remove yellow/diseased leaves; weed around plants; check for ants, aphids, whiteflies,
mites, thrips, caterpillars; check stakes.
Every 2-4 weeks
Feed lightly if plant is growing and fruiting; top up compost or mulch; inspect airflow and
crowding.
Before handling trays
Wash hands; clean tools; avoid tobacco handling before touching seedlings.
After handling diseased
plants
Wash hands, disinfect tools, and handle healthy plants separately.
