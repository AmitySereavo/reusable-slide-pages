Sunlight, Soil, and Water
Marigolds are best sold as easy, bright, sun-loving plants. The main rule is simple: give strong sun, good drainage, and do not
keep the roots soggy.
Need
Specific Target
Sunlight
At least 6 hours direct sun daily; 8 hours is even better for heavy blooming
Shade warning
Too much shade causes leafy plants and fewer flowers
Soil
Loose, fertile, well-draining soil; tolerates sand, loam, and clay if drainage is good
Water
Moderate and regular; water the soil, not the flowers
Wet-weather warning
Avoid overhead watering on tall plants because wet blooms and heavy stems can break or rot
Mulch
Thin mulch layer can reduce soil splash; keep mulch slightly away from stem
Drainage
Never allow trays, pots, or beds to stay waterlogged
For Jamaica and hot conditions: give morning and midday sun where possible, but keep new seedlings watered while roots
establish. Once established, marigolds tolerate short dry periods better than many tender herbs.

Harvesting and Flower Care
Marigolds can be sold as ornamental plants, companion plants, border plants, and cut flowers depending on the variety.
Goal
When / How
Keep plant blooming
Remove spent flowers every 5-10 days. This is called deadheading.
Cut flowers
Harvest when blooms are partly open to mostly open, depending on use. Cut early morning.
Dried flowers / seed saving
Allow selected flowers to dry on the plant, then collect when fully dry.
Market/display pots
Remove brown blooms and yellow leaves before delivery or sale.
Garden color
Plant in groups of 3, 5, 7, or long borders for stronger visual effect.
For large African marigolds, remove old wet blooms quickly during rainy weather to reduce gray mold and flower rot.

Small Farmer Notes
 Use marigolds as borders, row markers, pollinator-attraction strips, and ornamental companion plants around vegetables.
 Do not promise that marigolds will repel every pest. Customers must still inspect crops for whiteflies, aphids, mites, worms, and
disease.
 For nematode support, French marigolds are the type most often discussed. Best effect comes when grown as a dense cover
crop for about 2 months before planting susceptible crops, then incorporated or removed according to the system.
 For display beds, group compact French marigolds 6-10 inches apart and larger types 12-18 inches apart.
 Use staggered planting every 3-4 weeks if selling flowering marigolds over a longer season.
 Keep nursery trays weed-free and remove diseased plants quickly so problems do not spread.
Important wording for customers: marigold is a garden support plant, not a
replacement for good farming practice.

Home Garden Notes
 For customers buying 2-6 plants, recommend compact French or signet marigolds for pots and vegetable-bed edges.
 Plant near tomatoes, peppers, herbs, lettuce, cabbage, and fruiting vegetables as a color and companion border.
 Use pots at least 8-10 inches deep for compact types; larger types do better in 10-12+ inch containers.
 Place where plants receive at least 6 hours of sun.
 Water the soil when the top 1 inch begins to dry; avoid soaking flowers repeatedly.
 Remove old flowers weekly to keep plants attractive.
 In rainy periods, give more spacing and airflow to prevent rotting flowers and stem disease.

Clean Handling and Seedling Sanitation
Marigolds are usually tough plants, but clean handling protects nursery trays and customer seedlings.
 Wash hands before handling seedling trays, especially after touching diseased plants, soil, cigarettes, compost piles, or
chemical sprays.
 Do not rest cigarettes, food, dirty tools, or used plant labels on seedling trays.
 Use clean scissors or pruners when cutting flowers or removing diseased growth.
 Remove plants with severe wilting, rot at soil line, aster yellows symptoms, or heavy pest infestation from healthy stock.
 Avoid splashing soil onto leaves; many leaf diseases spread faster with wet, dirty foliage.
 Clean reusable trays and pots before planting new seedlings.

Hardening Off Seedlings
Hardening off prepares tray or small-pot seedlings for direct sun, wind, and outdoor heat. This section is near the back because
not every customer is transplanting from trays, but it is very important for customers who are.
Day
Exposure
Day 1-2
Bright shade or gentle morning sun for 2-3 hours
Day 3-4
Morning sun for 3-4 hours; protect from harsh afternoon heat
Day 5-6
Longer outdoor exposure; keep tray moist, not soggy
Day 7
Ready to transplant in early morning or late evening
If seedlings wilt badly during hardening off, move them back to shade, water gently, and continue more slowly.

Transplanting From Trays or Small Pots
Step
Instruction
1
Water seedlings 1-2 hours before transplanting.
2
Prepare the bed or pot first so roots are not exposed too long.
3
Transplant early morning before 9:00 a.m. or late evening after 4:30 p.m.
4
Keep the root ball together as much as possible.
5
Plant at the same depth as the pot/tray, or only slightly deeper if leggy.
6
Press soil gently around roots; do not compact hard.
7
Water immediately at soil level.
8
Give light shade for 1-2 days if the sun is very harsh.
9
Check daily for the first 3-5 days.
Spacing reminder: compact French types can be planted 6-10 inches apart; general mass planting 10-14 inches; taller
African/American types 12-18 inches apart.
