Tobacco warning: Cigarettes and other tobacco products can carry tobacco mosaic virus. Smokers or
anyone handling tobacco should wash hands well with soap before touching tomato seedlings, trays,
tools, leaves, stakes, or fruit. Do not smoke while handling tomato seedlings.
Do not touch tomato plants when leaves are wet unless necessary. Wet leaves spread bacterial and fungal
problems more easily.

5. From Tray to Field or Home Garden: Transplanting
This is the most important section for customers buying seedlings in trays.
Before removing seedlings from the tray:
 Water the tray 1-2 hours before transplanting.
 Transplant in the evening after 4:30 p.m. or on a cloudy day.
 Do not pull seedlings by the stem. Push or loosen from below and hold the root ball.
 Keep the root ball together as much as possible.
 Plant immediately; do not leave roots exposed to sun or wind.
Planting depth:
 Normal seedlings: plant slightly deeper than the tray plug, covering the plug by about 1 inch.
 Tall/leggy seedlings: remove the lowest leaves and bury part of the stem 2-3 inches deep to encourage extra
roots.
 Do not bury leaves under soil.
After transplanting:
 Water immediately.
 Shade lightly for 1-2 days if the sun is very harsh.
 Check every day for the first 7 days.
 Avoid strong fertilizer for the first 7-14 days unless plants are clearly hungry.

Shortcut warning: Seedlings moved from shade to full sun in one day can wilt, scorch, stall for 7-14

Simple field line: Plant - 2 ft - Plant - 2 ft - Plant. Leave 4-5 ft between rows so you can water, spray, weed,

Do not crowd slicing tomatoes. Crowding increases leaf wetness, early blight, Septoria leaf spot, whiteflies,
poor airflow, and small fruit.

For home gardeners, a simple test is to push one finger 1 inch into the soil. If that top inch is dry, water deeply.

10. Staking, Pruning, and Mulching
Large slicing tomatoes should be supported. Support improves airflow, keeps fruit off the soil, makes spraying
easier, and reduces damaged fruit.
Task
When
Specific instruction
Stake/cage
At transplanting or within 7 days
Install support early to avoid root damage later.
Tie plant
Every 7-10 days as it grows
Use soft tie. Do not cut into stem.

Remove leaves touching soil or within 6-8 inches of soil.

For small farmers, tying and pruning every week is easier than waiting until plants fall over. Heavy plants with
fruit can break quickly after rain or wind.

13. Harvesting Slicing Tomatoes
Harvest point
What it means
Breaker stage
First pink/red/yellow color shows at blossom end; good before heavy rain or transport
Firm ripe stage
Full color, still firm; best for salad/sandwich slicing
Soft ripe stage
Full color and soft; best used immediately
Harvest frequency
Every 2-4 days when plants are producing heavily
Field handling
Keep out of hot sun after picking. Do not pile too deep.
Home handling
Store ripe tomatoes at room temperature for best flavor; use quickly.
For small farmers: pick firm, clean fruit in the morning. Grade out cracked, insect-damaged, or diseased fruit so
they do not spoil good fruit in the container.
For home gardeners: harvesting slightly early at breaker stage can reduce cracking, bird damage, and
fruitworm damage.

14. Weekly Care Checklist + ParaLife Trees Support
Every 1-2 days:
 Check soil moisture.
 Look under leaves for whiteflies, aphids, mites, and thrips.
 Check for wilting, curling, new spots, and broken stems.
 Check flowers and young fruit for pest damage.
Every 7 days:
 Tie plants to stakes.
 Remove leaves touching soil.
 Remove spotted or diseased leaves.
 Weed around plants.
 Check mulch and airflow.
 Scout the whole row or garden bed.
