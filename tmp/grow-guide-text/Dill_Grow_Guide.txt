Main harvests

3. Sunlight, Soil, and Water
Need
Target / Customer Instruction
Sunlight
Give 6+ hours of light daily. In hot Jamaican conditions, morning sun with light afternoon shade
is safer for young seedlings.
Best location
Open, bright location with airflow. Avoid crowded, damp corners.
Soil
Loose, well-draining soil with compost. Dill dislikes heavy wet soil.
Soil pH
5.5-7.5; aim for 6.0-7.0 when possible.
Watering
Keep evenly moist during establishment. After that, water when the top 1 inch of soil begins to
dry.
Drainage
Do not let pots sit in standing water. Raise containers if the ground is waterlogged.
Mulch
Use a light mulch to reduce heat and splashing. Keep mulch slightly away from the stem.
Dill bolts faster when stressed. Consistent water, enough space, and gentle handling help keep leaves coming
longer.
4. Feeding Guide
Stage
Timing
Feeding Direction
At planting
Day 0
Mix compost into the soil. Avoid fresh manure or strong fertilizer touching
the roots.
After settling
7-10 days after planting
If leaves are pale, give a light liquid feed or mild balanced fertilizer.
Leaf production
Every 3-4 weeks if needed
Feed lightly only. Too much nitrogen can make weak, soft growth.
Before heavy harvest
2-3 days before cutting
Water evenly. Avoid heavy fertilizer right before harvest.
Seed production
When flowers form
Do not overfeed. Keep plants watered so seed heads fill properly.
Dill is not a heavy-feeding herb. Rich composted soil and light feeding are usually enough for home
gardens and small herb beds.

5. Harvesting Guide
Harvest Type
When / How
First light leaf harvest
Start when plants are about 6-8 inches tall and have enough leaves to keep growing.
Regular leaf harvest
Cut outer stems or tips every 7-14 days. Do not strip the whole plant at once.
Best time of day
Harvest early morning after leaves dry, or late evening after heat drops.
How much to remove
Remove no more than about 1/3 of the plant at one cutting if you want regrowth.
Flower heads for pickling
Harvest when flower umbrellas open and are still green/yellow, before seeds fully brown.
Seed harvest
Allow seed heads to turn brown. Cut stems and dry upside down in a paper bag.
Fresh handling
Keep harvested dill shaded and cool. Do not leave bunches in direct sun.
If the customer wants mostly leaves, cut flower stalks as soon as they appear. If the customer wants
seed, leave several strong plants to flower.

7. Bolting and Flower Stalks
Bolting means the plant is moving from leaf production into flowering and seed production. Dill naturally flowers, but
heat, root disturbance, crowding, and dry soil can make it happen too early.
What You See
Meaning
What To Do
Tall center stem rising
Plant is preparing to flower
Harvest leaves now if you want dill weed.
Yellow umbrella flower head
Normal flowering stage
Leave it for seed/pickling flowers or cut it to slow
seed production.
Small plant flowers very early
Stress bolting
Check heat, water, root crowding, and transplant
shock. Start a new batch if needed.
Leaves reduced after flowering
Plant energy shifted to seed
Harvest seeds later or replant fresh dill for leaves.
8. Small Farmer Notes
Topic
Recommendation
Planting batches
For steady leaf supply, plant or transplant in small batches every 2-3 weeks.
Spacing
Use 8-12 inches for leaf production; use 12-18 inches if allowing large plants/seed heads.
Rows
Use 12-24 inch row spacing depending on how you weed and harvest.
Shade strategy
Use afternoon shade or shade cloth during very hot periods to reduce stress bolting.
Harvest handling
Cut in the cool of the day. Keep bunches shaded and away from hot vehicle interiors.
Market quality
Remove yellow, dry, insect-damaged, or dirty stems before bunching.
9. Home Garden Notes
Situation
Recommendation
2-6 plants

Container growing

Harvest small amounts often instead of cutting the whole plant.

10. Clean Handling and Seedling Sanitation
Practice
Why It Matters
Wash hands before handling
seedlings
Reduces transfer of pests, disease organisms, and dirty residues.
Clean scissors/knife before harvest
Reduces disease spread between plants and keeps herbs cleaner for customers.
Do not handle sick plants then
healthy plants
Many plant problems move by hands, tools, splashing water, and insects.
Keep trays off dirty ground
Reduces soil splash, ants, slugs, and root disease risk.
Avoid smoking over seedling trays
Smoke, ash, and dirty hands are not good handling practice for customer food plants.
Remove heavily diseased plants
Protects the rest of the crop and reduces pest/disease buildup.
For food herbs, clean handling matters. Customers may eat dill fresh, so use clean water, clean
containers, and clean tools.
11. Hardening Off Seedlings
Hardening off prepares seedlings for direct sun, wind, and outdoor heat. Dill should be hardened off gently because
stress can trigger early flowering.
Day
Exposure
Day 1-2
Bright shade for 2-3 hours, then back to protected area.
Day 3-4
Morning sun for 2-4 hours, then bright shade.
Day 5-6
Longer morning sun plus bright afternoon shade. Keep cells moist.
Day 7
Ready to plant in early morning or late evening if seedling is not wilted.
Do not let tray cells dry out during hardening off. A dry cell can damage the taproot and push dill to bolt
early.

12. Transplanting From Trays or Small Pots
Dill is usually best direct-sown, but customers who buy seedlings can still transplant successfully if the root ball is
handled carefully.
Step
Instruction
1
Water seedlings 1-2 hours before transplanting.
2
Prepare holes before removing seedlings from the tray/pot.
3
Remove the seedling gently from the bottom of the cell. Do not pull by the stem.
4
Keep the root ball together. Do not tease, break, or wash the roots.
5
Plant at the same soil depth as the tray/pot. Do not bury the crown deeply.
6
Press soil gently around the root ball and water immediately.
7
Shade lightly for 1-2 days if weather is hot.
8
Check morning and evening for the first 3-5 days.
Best transplant timing: early morning before 8:30 a.m. or late evening after 4:30 p.m. Avoid midday
transplanting.

Signs
Action

Seedling collapses near soil line

Avoid overhead watering late.

Brown/black spots, often worse with wet
leaves
Remove spotted leaves. Water soil, not leaves.
Avoid crowding.
Aster yellows / viral-like

Remove badly affected plants. Control

Prevention matters more than rescue: clean trays, good drainage, enough space, dry foliage, and early
