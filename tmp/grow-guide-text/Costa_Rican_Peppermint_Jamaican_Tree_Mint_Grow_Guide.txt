Quick Numbers
Item
Target / Range
Likely botanical name
Satureja viminea / Micromeria viminea / Clinopodium vimineum
Common local names
Costa Rican peppermint, Jamaican mint tree, Jamaican mint bush, bush mint
Growth habit
Woody or semi-woody upright mint-family shrub
Leaf size
Small leaves, commonly about 0.5-2.5 cm long in technical descriptions
Plant height
About 3 ft commonly; can become 5-10 ft shrub/small tree in tropical conditions if unpruned
Sunlight
Full sun to part shade; best customer setting is morning sun plus bright shade in hot areas
Water
Regular water, but soil must drain well
Container
Start 4-6 inch plants in 1 gallon; larger plants need 3-5 gallon or bigger
Pruning
Pinch/cut every 2-4 weeks in active growth to keep bushy
Harvest
Cut leafy tips, not old bare wood; harvest lightly after transplanting
Identification note: this is not French thyme/Cuban oregano. French thyme/Cuban oregano has larger, thicker,
fuzzy/succulent leaves. This guide is for the small-leaf, woody Costa Rican peppermint/Jamaican tree mint type.

Sunlight, Soil, and Water
Need
Target
Sunlight
Full sun to part shade. In hot areas, use morning sun and afternoon shade.
Soil
Well-drained soil. Neutral to slightly alkaline soil is often recommended.
Water
Regular water, but never keep the root zone soggy.
Pot drainage
Drainage holes required. Do not leave pot sitting in water.
Airflow
Good airflow helps prevent leaf spots and stem rot.
Mulch
Light mulch is helpful, but keep it away from the stem base.
 For small customer pots, check moisture daily in hot weather.
 If the plant wilts in the afternoon but recovers by morning, it may need shade or a larger pot.
 If it wilts all day while soil is wet, check drainage and roots.

Flowering, Woody Stems, and Pruning
Pruning
 This plant should be pruned like a small herb shrub.
 Pinch soft tips every 2-4 weeks to keep it full.
 Do not keep cutting only the same side; shape the whole plant.
 Avoid cutting all the way into old bare wood unless plant is strong.
Woody Stems
 Woody stems are normal as the plant matures.
 If the plant gets bare at the bottom, prune gradually and let new shoots fill in.
 For customer pots, shift to a larger pot before the plant becomes root-bound.

Small Farmer Notes
 Grow mother plants in well-drained beds or 3-5 gallon pots.
 Space shrubs about 2-3 ft apart for hand harvest and airflow.
 Harvest leafy tips in light batches every 2-4 weeks.
 Keep mother plants pruned and clean for future cuttings.
 Do not overwater production plants; root disease is easier to prevent than fix.
 Label clearly as Costa Rican Peppermint / Jamaican Tree Mint - not French thyme.

Home Garden Notes
 For 1-3 plants, grow in pots where the customer can manage water and pruning.
 Start small plants in 1 gallon pots; move larger plants to 3-5 gallon pots.
 Use morning sun with afternoon shade if leaves burn.
 Prune tips often for tea/kitchen use and a bushy shape.
 Do not let the pot stay in a saucer of water.

Clean Handling and Seedling Sanitation
 Wash hands before handling plants, especially after handling diseased plants or tobacco/cigarettes.
 Use clean scissors for harvest and pruning.
 Do not take cuttings from plants with leaf spots, mites, or mealybugs.
 Keep pot surfaces clean by removing fallen leaves.
 When propagating, use clean pots and well-drained medium.
Clean handling is especially important if plants are being sold to customers as starter plants.

 Keep root ball moist during hardening off.
 If leaves wilt badly, return plant to shade and water gently.
 Do not move shade-grown plants straight to full hot sun.

Transplanting From Small Pots
 Water the plant 1-2 hours before transplanting.
 Prepare a well-draining potting mix before removing the plant.
 Move 4-6 inch sale plants into 1 gallon pots if the customer wants steady harvest.
 Keep the root ball together.
 Plant at the same depth as the original pot.
 Water after transplanting and keep in bright shade for 1-2 days.
 Do not fertilize heavily right after transplanting.

Common Pests
Pest
Signs
Action
Mealybugs
White cottony clusters on stem joints and leaf nodes
Wipe off small groups; prune bad areas; repeat treatment.
Aphids
Soft insects on new growth; sticky leaves
Spray off and use mild soap solution; repeat 3-5 days.
Whiteflies
Tiny white insects under leaves
Spray undersides; use yellow sticky traps.
Spider mites
Fine webbing, speckled dry leaves
Rinse leaves; prune damage; avoid drought stress.
Scale insects
Small bumps on stems/leaves; sticky residue
Prune heavily infested parts; wipe stems; monitor new
growth.
