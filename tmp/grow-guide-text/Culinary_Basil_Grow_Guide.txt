3. Basil Types: Identification and Differences
Most basils like warm weather, bright light, loose well-draining soil, and regular harvesting. The differences below
help customers identify what they bought and how the flavor or growth habit may differ.
Basil Type
How To Identify It
Main Use / Difference
Italian Sweet Basil
Soft green leaves, sweet clove-like aroma, tender
stems, broad oval leaves.
Good all-purpose cooking basil for sauces,
salads, seasoning, and fresh use.
Genovese Basil
Large glossy cupped leaves, strong sweet basil
aroma, classic pesto type.
Best known for pesto and Italian-style sauces.
Often more sensitive to basil downy mildew
than some specialty basils.
Thai Basil
Narrower leaves, firmer texture, purple stems,
purple flower spikes, anise/licorice scent.
Holds flavor well in cooked dishes. Usually
more heat-tolerant and sturdier than sweet
basil.
Lemon Basil
Smaller to medium leaves, lemon/citrus scent,
often lighter green and more delicate.
Good for teas, fish/vegetable seasoning, salads,
and light sauces. Often shows more resistance
to some basil disease issues than classic sweet
basil.
Cinnamon Basil
Green leaves, purple stems/flowers, warm spicy
cinnamon/clove scent.
Good for tea, desserts, infused drinks, and
scented garden use.
Purple Basil / Dark
Opal
Purple to burgundy leaves, decorative color,
basil/clove scent.
Good for garnish, colored vinegar, salads, and
ornamental edible planting. Color improves with
strong light.
Holy Basil / Tulsi
More textured or slightly hairy leaves, spicy clove
scent, can become woody and branchy.
Used more for tea/traditional herb use. Grows
more shrub-like than soft Italian basil.
African Blue /
Perennial Basil
Types
Woody stems, larger bush, often purple flowers,
strong scent, can live longer in warm climates.
Best for longer-term herb hedge/pollinator plant.
Needs more pruning and more space.

4. Sunlight, Soil, and Water
Need
Target / Customer Instruction
Sunlight
Give 4-6+ hours daily. In hot Jamaican conditions, morning sun plus light afternoon shade
works well.
Best location
Bright, warm spot with good airflow and protection from waterlogged soil.
Soil
Loose, rich, well-draining soil with compost. Avoid compacted soil that stays wet.
Soil pH
6.0-7.5
Watering
Water when the top 1 inch of soil begins to dry. Keep evenly moist, not soggy.
Containers
Use drainage holes. Do not let the pot sit in water. In hot weather, containers may need
daily checking.
Mulch
Use light mulch to keep roots cooler and reduce soil splash. Keep mulch slightly away from
the stem.
Water at soil level where possible. Wet leaves, crowding, and poor airflow increase fungal disease
pressure, especially downy mildew and leaf spots.
5. Feeding Guide
Stage
Timing
Feeding Direction
At planting
Day 0
Mix compost into the soil. Avoid strong fertilizer directly against roots.
After settling
7-10 days after planting
Use light liquid feed or mild balanced fertilizer if leaves look pale.
Active growth
Every 2-4 weeks
Feed lightly. Basil grows best with steady nutrition, not heavy feeding.
Before harvest
Final 2-3 days before
cutting
Focus on clean watering and healthy leaves. Avoid heavy fertilizer
right before harvest.
Potted basil
Ongoing
Because nutrients wash out of pots faster, light feeding every 2-3
weeks may help.
Avoid overfeeding nitrogen. Too much soft leafy growth can attract pests and reduce strong flavor.

6. Harvesting Guide
Harvest Point
What To Do
First harvest
Start when plant is 6-8 inches tall and has 4-6 pairs of leaves.
How to cut
Cut just above a pair of leaves/nodes. This encourages two new shoots.
How much to remove
Do not remove more than about 1/3 of the plant at one time.
Harvest frequency
Harvest every 7-14 days during strong growth.
Best time
Early morning after dew dries, or late evening after heat drops.
Flower buds
Pinch flower buds every 3-7 days to keep the plant producing leaves.
Market bunches
Harvest clean stems, keep shaded, and avoid leaving harvested basil in hot sun.
For better branching, customers should not just pick single leaves from the bottom every time.
Cutting stem tips above a leaf pair keeps basil bushier and more productive.

8. Bolting and Flower Stalks
Basil naturally tries to flower as it matures. Flowering is not a disaster, but it can make leaf production slow down
and leaf flavor stronger or harsher. Customers should pinch flower tips early if they want more leaves.
What You See
Meaning
What To Do
Small bud cluster at stem tip
Plant is starting to flower
Pinch or cut the tip above a leaf pair.
Long flower spikes
Plant is focusing on flowers/seeds
Cut back by 1/3 if plant is healthy. Water and feed
lightly after pruning.
Bees visiting flowers
Normal, especially Thai, holy, and
perennial basil
Leave some flowers if plant is for pollinators; cut
flowers if plant is for leaf harvest.
Leaves getting smaller
Plant is older, stressed, or flowering
Prune tips, improve water/light, or restart with
fresh seedlings.
9. Small Farmer Notes
Area
Recommendation
Spacing
Use 12-18 inches between plants for most basil; 18-24 inches for larger perennial basil types.
Batch planting
Plant new batches every 2-4 weeks for steady sales and fresh bundles.
Harvest rotation
Cut from different rows or sections every 7-14 days. Do not strip plants bare.
Disease prevention
Avoid overhead watering late in the day. Increase row airflow. Do not harvest wet plants.
Market handling
Keep harvested basil shaded. Basil bruises and wilts quickly in hot sun.
Sanitation
Use clean scissors/knife. Remove diseased plants early. Do not compost severe downy mildew
plants near production.

10. Home Garden Notes
Customer Type
Simple Instruction
2-6 plants in pots
Use 1-3 gallon pots, drainage holes, morning sun, and regular tip harvesting.
Kitchen garden bed
Space 12-18 inches apart. Plant close enough for easy harvest but not so close that leaves stay
wet.
Balcony / veranda
Give bright light. Rotate pots weekly. Watch moisture because pots dry faster.
Tea/herbal use
Holy basil, lemon basil, cinnamon basil, and some perennial types are especially useful for teas
and scented harvests.
Pesto/sauce use
Genovese and Italian sweet basil are the main choices for classic pesto and sauces.
11. Clean Handling and Seedling Sanitation
Clean handling matters because basil leaves are eaten fresh and diseases spread easily through wet leaves, dirty
tools, infected plant material, and crowded growing areas.
Sanitation Step
Why It Matters
Wash hands before handling plants
Reduces spread of plant diseases and keeps harvest cleaner.
Use clean scissors or knife
Reduces transfer of disease between plants.
Do not harvest wet plants
Wet leaves bruise easily and spread fungal spores faster.
Remove diseased leaves/plants
Reduces spores and keeps problems from spreading.
Keep trays and pots off dirty
ground
Reduces soil splash and root disease.
Do not smoke over seedlings or
rest cigarettes on trays
Good nursery hygiene; also important around vegetable/herb seedlings generally.

12. Hardening Off Seedlings
This section is mainly for customers buying seedlings from trays or small pots. Potted plants already growing
outdoors may need less hardening off, but tray seedlings should still be protected from sudden hot sun.
Day
Exposure
Day 1-2
Bright shade for 2-3 hours. Keep cells moist.
Day 3-4
Morning sun for 2-4 hours, then shade.
Day 5-6
Morning sun plus bright afternoon shade. Watch for wilting.
Day 7
Ready to plant early morning or late evening.
Do not move basil seedlings straight from shade or nursery protection into full hot sun for the
whole day. Sudden sun and dry roots can stunt or kill soft seedlings.
13. Transplanting From Trays or Small Pots
Step
Instruction
1
Water the seedlings 1-2 hours before transplanting.
2
Prepare the hole or container before removing the plant.
3
Remove seedling gently and keep the root ball together.
4
Plant at the same soil level as the tray/pot. Do not bury leaves.
5
Press soil gently around roots and water immediately.
6
Give light shade for 1-2 days if the sun is harsh.
7
Check daily for the first 3-5 days.

14. Common Pests
Pest
Signs
Action
Aphids
Soft insects on new tips, curled leaves,
sticky honeydew.
Spray off with water or mild soap solution. Repeat every
3-5 days.
Whiteflies
Tiny white insects fly when disturbed;
yellowing/sticky leaves.
Spray undersides, use sticky traps, remove infested
leaves.
Mealybugs
White cottony clusters on stems or leaf
joints.
Wipe off small groups; spray mild soap solution.
Spider mites
Fine webbing, speckled/dusty leaves,
worse in dry heat.
Rinse undersides, reduce drought stress, repeat
treatment.
Caterpillars
Chewed holes, droppings, missing leaf
edges.
Handpick early morning/evening; remove damaged
leaves.
Leafminers
White winding tunnels inside leaves.
Remove affected leaves and control weeds nearby.
15. Common Diseases
Disease
Signs
Action
Basil downy mildew
Yellow patches on top of leaves; gray/purple
fuzzy growth under leaves.
Remove infected plants/leaves. Keep leaves dry.
Improve airflow and spacing.
Fusarium wilt
Sweet basil may grow normally until 8-12
inches, then wilt, stunt, and show stem
discoloration.
Remove infected plants. Do not reuse
contaminated soil for basil. Use resistant varieties
when possible.
Leaf spot
Brown/black spots, often worse with wet leaves
and poor airflow.
Remove affected leaves. Water soil, not leaves.
Improve spacing.
Root rot
Wilting with wet soil, weak plant, dark roots,
bad soil smell.
Reduce watering. Improve drainage. Repot if
container soil stays soggy.
Damping off
Young seedlings collapse at soil line.
Use clean trays/media. Avoid overwatering and
overcrowding.
Gray mold / Botrytis
Soft gray fuzzy mold on damaged or wet plant
tissue.
Remove affected tissue. Improve airflow and
avoid wet foliage.
