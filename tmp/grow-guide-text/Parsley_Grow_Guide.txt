3. Parsley Type Identification
Type
How To Identify It
Use / Difference
Curly parsley
Tightly curled, frilly leaves; compact look
Good for garnish, bunching, and fresh use. Holds
shape well.
Flat-leaf / Italian parsley
Flat, deeply cut leaves; looks similar to cilantro but
usually thicker and less delicate
Often stronger flavor; good for cooking, sauces,
seasoning, and chopping.
Root parsley
Leaves look parsley-like, but plant is grown for a
thick white root
Less common for herb pots; needs deeper soil and
more time.
Cilantro comparison
Cilantro leaves are usually softer and more
rounded/lacy; smell is very different
Do not label cilantro as parsley. Smell the leaves if
unsure.
For customer guides, title it "Parsley Grow Guide" and include both curly and flat-leaf parsley unless you are selling
one named type only.
4. Sunlight, Soil, and Water
Need
Target / Customer Instruction
Sunlight
Give 4-6+ hours daily. In hot Jamaican conditions, morning sun and light afternoon shade help
keep leaves tender.
Best location
Bright area with airflow. Avoid crowded, damp corners.
Soil
Loose, rich, well-draining soil with compost. Parsley prefers steady moisture but not swampy
roots.
Soil pH
Aim for 6.0-7.0 when possible.
Watering
Keep evenly moist. Water when the top 1 inch of soil begins to dry.
Containers
Use drainage holes. Do not let pots sit in standing water.
Mulch
Use light mulch in hot beds. Keep mulch slightly away from the crown.
Parsley grows best when it does not repeatedly wilt. The goal is steady moisture, not constant wetness.

5. Feeding Guide
Stage
Timing
Feeding Direction
At planting
Day 0
Mix compost into the soil. Avoid fresh manure or strong fertilizer touching
roots.
After settling
7-10 days after planting
If leaves are pale, apply a light liquid feed or mild balanced fertilizer.
Leaf production
Every 3-4 weeks if needed
Feed lightly for steady leaf growth. Do not overfeed.
Container plants
Every 2-4 weeks if growth
slows
Containers lose nutrients faster; use gentle feeding and steady watering.
Before harvest
Final 3-5 days
Focus on clean water and healthy leaves, not heavy fertilizer.
Parsley is grown for leaves, so it needs enough nitrogen for green growth, but too much fertilizer can make weak soft
growth and reduce quality.
6. Harvesting Guide
Harvest Type
When / How
First light harvest
When plants have several strong outer stems; often 30-45 days after transplant.
Regular harvest
Cut outer stalks close to the base. Leave the center growing point to keep producing.
Harvest frequency
Every 7-14 days once plant is established and growing strongly.
How much to remove
Do not remove more than about 1/3 of the plant at one cutting if you want regrowth.
Best time of day
Harvest early morning after leaves dry, or late evening after heat drops.
Fresh handling
Keep parsley shaded and cool after cutting. Do not leave bunches in hot sun.
End of plant cycle
When a tall flower stalk appears, leaf quality usually declines. Replace with new seedlings.
Best customer instruction: cut whole outer stems near the base, not just the leaf tips. This encourages
cleaner regrowth from the center.

8. Flower Stalks, Bolting, and Year 2 Growth
Parsley is a biennial. In the first year it focuses on leaves. In the second year it naturally sends up a tall flower stalk,
flowers, makes seed, and then the plant declines.
What You See
Meaning
What To Do

Small yellow-green flower

flowers.

Replace with new plants for fresh bunching.
Early stalk in young plant

seedlings next batch.

9. Small Farmer Notes
Topic
Recommendation
Planting batches
For steady supply, plant or transplant in batches every 3-4 weeks.
Spacing
Use 8-12 inches between plants and 12-18 inches between rows for hand harvest.
Bed setup
Use raised beds where soil holds water. Keep rows weeded for airflow.
Shade strategy
In hot periods, use afternoon shade or 30-40% shade cloth for tender leaves.
Harvest handling
Cut in the cool of the day. Keep bunches shaded, clean, and cool.
Market quality
Remove yellow, dry, insect-damaged, muddy, or diseased stems before bunching.
10. Home Garden Notes
Situation
Recommendation
2-6 plants
Plant 8-12 inches apart in a bright bed or container.
Container growing
Use a pot at least 8-10 inches deep with drainage holes.
Kitchen use
Harvest outer stems often instead of cutting the whole plant.
Hot patio
Give morning sun and shade after midday. Check water daily.
Continuous supply
Start new parsley every few months, because older plants eventually flower and decline.

11. Clean Handling and Seedling Sanitation
Practice
Why It Matters
Wash hands before handling
seedlings
Reduces transfer of pests, disease organisms, and dirty residues.
Clean scissors/knife before harvest
Reduces disease spread and keeps fresh herbs cleaner.
Do not handle sick plants then
healthy plants
Many plant problems move by hands, tools, splashing water, and insects.
Keep trays off dirty ground
Reduces soil splash, ants, slugs, and root disease risk.
Avoid smoking over seedling trays
Smoke, ash, and dirty hands are poor handling practice for fresh herbs.
Remove heavily diseased plants
Protects the rest of the crop and reduces pest/disease buildup.
Parsley is often eaten fresh, so clean handling matters: clean water, clean containers, clean tools, and
clean hands.
12. Hardening Off Seedlings
Hardening off prepares parsley seedlings for direct sun, wind, and outdoor heat. This is useful for tray seedlings and
small pots before they are planted out.
Day
Exposure
Day 1-2
Bright shade for 2-3 hours, then back to protected area.
Day 3-4
Morning sun for 2-4 hours, then bright shade.
Day 5-6
Longer morning sun plus bright afternoon shade. Keep cells moist.
Day 7
Ready to plant in early morning or late evening if seedling is not wilted.
Do not let tray cells dry out during hardening off. Small parsley seedlings recover slowly after severe wilt.

13. Transplanting From Trays or Small Pots
Step
Instruction
1
Water seedlings 1-2 hours before transplanting.
2
Prepare holes before removing seedlings from the tray/pot.
3
Remove the seedling gently from the bottom of the cell. Do not pull by the leaves.
4
Keep the root ball together as much as possible. Parsley can be slow after root disturbance.
5
Plant at the same soil depth as the tray/pot. Do not bury the crown deeply.
6
Press soil gently around the root ball and water immediately.
7
Shade lightly for 1-2 days if the weather is hot.
8
Check morning and evening for the first 3-5 days.
Best transplant timing: early morning before 8:30 a.m. or late evening after 4:30 p.m. Avoid midday
transplanting.

15. Common Diseases
Disease / Problem
Signs
Action
Damping off
Seedling collapses near soil line
Avoid overwatering. Improve airflow. Use clean
media. Remove collapsed plants.
Root rot
Wilting with wet soil; yellowing; weak roots
Improve drainage. Reduce watering. Repot if
container stays soggy.
Powdery mildew
White powdery coating on leaves
Increase airflow and spacing. Remove bad leaves.
Avoid wet foliage late.
Leaf spots
Brown/black spots, often worse with wet
leaves
Remove spotted leaves. Water soil, not leaves.
Avoid crowding.
Crown rot
Soft rot at base/crown; plant collapses
Keep crown dry, improve drainage, remove badly
affected plants.
Aster yellows / viral-like
disorders
Yellowing, stunting, distorted growth or
abnormal flowers
Remove badly affected plants. Control
leafhoppers/aphids and weeds.
Prevention matters more than rescue: clean trays, good drainage, enough space, dry foliage, and early
pest control are the best disease protection.
