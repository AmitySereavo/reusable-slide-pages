3. Sunlight, Soil, and Water
Need
Target / Customer Instruction
Sunlight
Give 6-8+ hours of sun daily. In very hot lowland conditions, young plants can use morning sun
plus light afternoon shade for the first 1-2 weeks.
Best location
Bright, open, breezy area. Avoid damp corners with poor airflow.
Soil texture
Loose, gritty, well-draining soil. Rosemary dislikes heavy clay or mud-like soil.
Drainage test
After watering, excess water should drain away within minutes, not sit around the roots.
Container mix
Use potting mix plus extra drainage material such as coarse sand, perlite, pumice, or fine gravel.
Watering - young plant
Keep lightly moist while roots establish for 2-4 weeks. Do not flood.
Watering - established plant
Let the top 1-2 inches of soil dry before watering again. Water deeply, then allow drainage.
Mulch
Use a thin mineral mulch or very light organic mulch. Keep mulch 1-2 inches away from the
stem.
Do not place rosemary in a saucer that holds water. If using a decorative pot, make sure the inside pot can
drain freely.

Active growth

Container plants
Every 6-8 weeks in active

Containers lose nutrients faster, but still avoid overfeeding.
Before heavy

1-2 weeks before

Overfeeding rosemary can create soft growth that is more pest-prone and less aromatic. Use light feeding
and focus more on drainage, sun, and pruning.

5. Harvesting Guide
Harvest Type
When / How
First light harvest
About 4-8 weeks after transplant, only if the plant has strong new growth.
Regular harvest
Cut young green tips 3-6 inches long as needed.
How much to remove
Do not remove more than 1/3 of the green growth at one time.
Best time
Morning after dew dries, or late afternoon after the strongest heat.
Where to cut
Cut above a leaf pair or side shoot to encourage branching.
Drying
Tie small bundles and hang in a shaded, airy place for 1-2 weeks, or dry on a clean rack.
Storage
Store fully dried leaves in a clean airtight container away from heat and sunlight.
For sales or home use, frequent light tip harvest keeps rosemary bushy. Heavy cutting into old woody
stems can leave bare patches that recover slowly or not at all.

Rosemary does not bolt like lettuce or basil, but it can become woody with age. It may also flower naturally, especially
when mature and grown in bright sun.

Plant becomes woody inside

into bare wood.
Long thin growth

Good rosemary pruning is small and regular: take green tips often rather than making one heavy cut.
8. Small Farmer Notes
Topic
Recommendation
Site choice
Use the driest, best-drained section of the farm. Avoid low areas that stay wet after rain.
Spacing
Use 24-36 inches between plants. Use 36-48 inches between rows for walking, weeding, and
harvest.
Drainage
Raised beds or ridges are better in heavy soil or wet seasons.
Harvest schedule
Light tip harvest every 3-6 weeks once plants are established and actively growing.
Plant replacement
Keep backup plants from cuttings because rosemary can decline quickly if roots stay wet.
Market quality
Sell clean, firm, aromatic green tips. Remove blackened, yellow, or pest-damaged stems.
9. Home Garden Notes
Situation
Recommendation
1-6 plants
Plant in sunny, airy places. Keep at least 18-24 inches between plants.
Container growing
Use a pot with drainage holes. For long-term growth, move up to 3-5 gallons or larger.
Patio/balcony
Avoid saucers full of water. Give 6+ hours of light if possible.
Kitchen use
Cut small tips often instead of stripping the whole plant.
Rainy season
Move potted rosemary where rain will not keep the soil soaked every day.

10. Clean Handling and Seedling Sanitation
Rosemary is often used fresh in food, so clean handling matters for both plant health and customer safety.
Practice
Why It Matters
Wash hands before handling plants
Reduces spread of pests, disease, soil organisms, and food contamination.
Use clean scissors/pruners
Dirty tools can spread stem and leaf diseases from plant to plant.
Do not handle diseased plants then
healthy plants
Work from healthy plants first, problem plants last. Wash hands/tools after.
Avoid splashing dirty soil onto leaves
Soil splash can carry disease organisms onto harvestable stems.
Keep harvest bunches off the ground
Improves cleanliness and market quality.
Do not smoke directly over plants
Smoke, ash, and dirty hands reduce cleanliness; wash hands before harvesting.
For food herbs, clean hands, clean tools, and clean harvest containers are part of the product quality.
11. Hardening Off Before Planting
Hardening off prepares potted rosemary or young plants for stronger sun, wind, and outdoor conditions.
Day
Exposure
Day 1-2
Bright shade or morning sun for 2-3 hours. Keep soil lightly moist.
Day 3-4
Morning sun for 3-5 hours. Protect from heavy midday heat or strong wind.
Day 5-6
Longer outdoor exposure. Check that leaves stay firm and soil drains well.
Day 7
Plant into final position early morning or late afternoon.
Do not move rosemary straight from shade or nursery protection into full all-day sun and wind. A 5-7 day
hardening period reduces shock.
12. Transplanting from Small Pots
Step
Instruction
1
Water the plant 1-2 hours before transplanting so the root ball holds together.
2
Prepare the hole or container first. Make it slightly wider than the root ball.
3
Remove the plant gently. Do not pull hard by the stem.
4
Keep the root ball together. Loosen only circling outer roots if the plant is root-bound.
5
Plant at the same depth as it was growing in the pot. Do not bury the woody stem deeply.
6
Backfill with well-draining soil and press gently. Do not compact hard.
7
Water once to settle the soil, then let excess water drain away.
8
Protect from extreme sun/rain for 1-2 days, then return to strong light.
9
Do not fertilize heavily for the first 2-3 weeks after transplanting.
The biggest transplanting mistake with rosemary is planting into heavy wet soil or a pot with poor drainage.
Drainage matters more than rich soil.

13. Common Pests
Pest
Signs
What To Do
Spider mites
Tiny speckles, dry-looking leaves, fine webbing
Rinse leaf undersides. Improve plant vigor. Repeat
every 3-5 days.
Mealybugs
White cottony clusters on stems or leaf joints
Wipe off. Prune bad tips. Use mild soap spray in cool
hours.
Scale insects
Hard bumps on stems/leaves, sticky residue
Scrape/wipe small patches. Prune heavy infestations.
Improve airflow.
Aphids
Soft insects on new tips, sticky leaves, ants
Spray with water first. Use mild soap spray every 3-5
days if needed.
Whiteflies
Tiny white flying insects, yellowing/sticky leaves
Spray under leaves. Use yellow sticky traps. Keep
weeds down.
Thrips
Silvery streaks, distorted tips/flowers
Remove damaged tips. Use sticky traps and spray
carefully under leaves.
Rosemary usually has fewer pest problems when grown in full sun, with airflow, and without overwatering
or overfeeding.
14. Common Diseases
Disease / Problem
Signs
What To Do
Root rot
Wilting with wet soil, black roots, plant
decline
Stop watering. Improve drainage. Repot if possible.
Remove badly affected plants.
Stem rot
Black or soft lower stems
Reduce moisture around stem. Remove affected stems.
Improve airflow.
Powdery mildew
White powdery coating on leaves
Increase sun and airflow. Avoid overhead watering.
Remove affected tips.
Leaf spot
Brown/black spots, often worse in damp
conditions
Remove affected leaves. Avoid wet foliage. Space
plants better.
Botrytis/grey mold
Grey fuzzy mold on crowded wet growth
Prune for airflow. Remove infected material. Keep
leaves dry.
Nutrient/root stress
Yellowing and slow growth without clear
pests
Check root health, drainage, light, and pot size before
feeding.
When rosemary is declining, always check drainage before adding fertilizer. Feeding a plant with rotting
roots usually makes the problem worse.
