3. Sunlight, Soil, and Water
Sweet peppers need strong light, warm soil, and steady moisture. Most poor fruiting comes from low sun, root
stress, uneven watering, or too much nitrogen.
Need
Target
Customer Notes
Sunlight
6-10 hours daily
Best fruiting usually comes with 8-10 hours. In very hot areas, morning sun
plus light afternoon relief can reduce stress.
Soil texture
Loose, fertile, well-draining
Avoid soil that stays waterlogged after rain. Raised beds help in wet areas.
Soil pH
About 6.0-6.8
Compost improves soil structure and root health.
Water
1-2 inches per week
Containers may need water more often. Keep watering even once flowers and
fruit appear.
Mulch
2-3 inches thick
Keep mulch slightly away from stem. Mulch reduces heat stress, weeds, and
water swings.
Airflow
Leaves should dry quickly
Good spacing lowers leaf spot and mildew pressure.
Do not keep pepper roots constantly wet. Peppers like moisture, but they do not like soggy root zones.

4. Feeding Guide
Stage
Timing
Feeding Direction
At planting
Day 0
Mix compost into bed or potting mix. Use a balanced fertilizer lightly
if soil is weak.
After settling
10-14 days after transplant
Feed lightly once plant is no longer wilted. Avoid strong fertilizer on
stressed seedlings.
Vegetative growth
Weeks 2-5 after transplant
Support steady leaf and root growth, but do not push soft, overly
leafy growth.
Flowering begins
Often 4-7 weeks after
transplant
Reduce heavy nitrogen. Support phosphorus, potassium, calcium,
and consistent watering.
Fruiting stage
Fruit visible
Feed lightly every 2-4 weeks in pots or 3-5 weeks in ground, based
on plant color and growth.
Harvesting stage
Ongoing
Keep water consistent and avoid sudden heavy nitrogen that can
reduce fruiting balance.
Too much nitrogen can give a large green plant with fewer flowers and fruit. Use compost, balanced
feeding, and fruiting support once flowers begin.

5. Harvesting Guide
Harvest Stage
When To Harvest
Notes
Green mature
Fruit is full size, firm, glossy, and deep green
Many bell peppers are ready around 70-80 days after
transplanting.
Color ripe
Fruit changes to red, yellow, orange, purple, or

Usually 2-3 weeks after the green mature stage;

Leaf / Plant Look
Possible Cause
What To Do
Medium to deep green leaves
Healthy plant
Continue steady watering, feeding, sunlight, and pest

Problem
Likely Cause
Action
Flowers drop before fruit

nitrogen.
Plant flowers but fruit is poor
Stress, poor pollination, pest damage, low

Check pests in flowers, feed lightly for fruiting, and

Poor nutrition, small pot, irregular water,
variety type

Calcium transport problem often from uneven

8. Small Farmer Notes
Practice
Recommended Approach
Spacing
Use 18-24 inches in-row and 30-36 inches between rows for airflow and harvest access.
Raised beds
Use raised beds where soil holds water or rain is heavy.
Mulch
Use dry grass, weed mat, plastic mulch, or organic mulch to reduce weeds and water swings.
Staking
Stake or string plants where wind, heavy fruit, or soft soil may cause lodging.
Irrigation
Drip or careful soil-level watering lowers leaf wetness and disease spread.
Crop rotation
Avoid planting peppers after peppers, tomato, eggplant, or Irish potato in the same bed when disease
was present.
Record keeping
Track date planted, variety, feed dates, first flowering, first harvest, pest treatment, and yield.

9. Home Garden Notes
Situation
Simple Guidance
2-6 plants
Plant 18-24 inches apart or use one 5-gallon pot per plant.
Balcony/patio
Use 5-gallon or larger pot with drainage holes; full sun is best.
Small yard
Mulch and stake plants to keep fruit clean and branches supported.
Rainy periods
Reduce watering and improve drainage. Watch leaf spots.
Hot sunny areas
Keep soil moist, mulch heavily, and avoid pruning away too many leaves.
Harvest use
Pick green for cooking or wait for full color for sweeter flavor.
For the home gardener, the biggest success factors are pot size, sunlight, drainage, and consistent
watering once fruit starts.

10. Clean Handling and Seedling Sanitation
Peppers can be affected by plant viruses and leaf diseases that spread by insects, tools, hands, plant sap, and
contaminated material. Clean handling lowers risk.
Do This
Why It Matters
Wash hands before handling seedlings
Reduces transfer of plant disease organisms.
Keep cigarettes and tobacco away from
seedlings
Tobacco products can be associated with tobacco mosaic virus risk in peppers and
tomato.
Do not smoke and then handle seedlings
without washing hands
Wash with soap and water before touching peppers.
Clean scissors/pruners before use
Reduces spread of bacteria, fungi, and viruses between plants.
Do not work plants when leaves are wet
Wet leaves spread bacterial and fungal leaf spots more easily.
Remove sick leaves from the garden
Do not leave diseased leaves under plants.
Customer warning: wash hands after smoking or handling cigarettes before touching pepper seedlings
or plants.

11. Hardening Off Seedlings
Hardening off prepares seedlings from trays or protected areas for stronger sun, wind, and outdoor stress. Do this
before final planting when possible.
Day
Exposure
Day 1-2
Bright shade or gentle morning light for 2-3 hours. Keep tray moist.
Day 3-4
Morning sun for 3-4 hours, then shade. Avoid midday heat.
Day 5-6
Morning sun plus longer outdoor exposure. Protect from heavy rain and strong wind.
Day 7-10
Ready for planting if seedlings stay upright and do not wilt badly.
Do not take tray-grown pepper seedlings straight from shade into full hot sun. Sudden stress can slow
growth and delay fruiting.

12. Transplanting from Trays or Small Pots
Step
Instruction
1
Water seedlings 1-2 hours before transplanting.
2
Prepare holes or pots before removing seedlings from trays.
3
Handle by root ball or leaves, not by crushing the stem.
4
Keep root ball together as much as possible.
5
Plant at the same depth as the tray/pot soil level.
6
Press soil gently around roots; do not pack hard.
7
Water immediately at soil level.
8
Shade lightly for 1-2 days if sun is harsh.
9
Avoid strong fertilizer for the first few days if plant is wilted.
10
Check morning and evening for the first 3-5 days.
Pepper seedlings can suffer transplant shock. Careful handling and steady moisture help them settle
faster.

13. Common Pests
Pest
Signs
Action
Whiteflies
Tiny white insects under leaves, yellowing,
sticky residue
Spray under leaves, use yellow sticky traps, control
weeds, repeat every 3-5 days.
Aphids
Soft insects on new growth, curling, sticky
leaves, ants
Spray with water, use mild soap spray, control ants, repeat
every 3-5 days.
Thrips
Silvery/bronze marks, distorted flowers/leaves,
poor fruit shape
Remove damaged growth, use sticky traps, spray carefully
around new growth/flowers.
Spider mites
Fine speckling, dusty leaves, webbing, worse
in hot/dry weather
Spray leaf undersides, reduce drought stress, remove
badly damaged leaves.
Pepper weevil
Small holes, fruit drop, larvae inside fruit
Remove fallen/damaged fruit, keep field clean, monitor
early.
Cutworms
Young seedlings cut at soil line
Check early morning, use collars/barriers, remove weeds
and debris before planting.
Caterpillars/worms
Chewed leaves or fruit, black droppings
Handpick, check evening/early morning, remove damaged
fruit.

14. Common Diseases and Disorders
Issue
Signs
First Response
Bacterial spot
Small dark leaf spots, yellow halos, spots on
fruit
Avoid wet leaves, improve spacing, remove affected
leaves, do not work plants wet.
Anthracnose
Sunken circular fruit spots, often on ripening
fruit
Remove infected fruit, avoid overhead watering,
harvest promptly.
Cercospora leaf spot
Circular leaf spots with light centers
Remove bad leaves, improve airflow, reduce leaf
wetness.
Phytophthora blight/root rot
Wilting, stem/root rot, collapse in wet soil
Improve drainage, remove infected plants, avoid
waterlogged beds.
Tobacco mosaic virus
Mottled/mosaic leaves, stunting, distorted
growth
Remove badly infected plants, wash hands, avoid
tobacco contamination.
Blossom-end rot
Dark sunken bottom of fruit
Maintain even water, mulch, avoid root injury and
over-fertilizing nitrogen.
Sunscald
Pale/tan burned patches on exposed fruit
Maintain healthy leaf cover and reduce excessive
pruning.
