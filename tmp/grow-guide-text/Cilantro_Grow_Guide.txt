3. Identification: Cilantro, Coriander and Culantro
 Name
 How To Identify It
 Main Use
Cilantro
Soft, flat, parsley-like leaves with a fresh sharp smell. Older
plants make thinner, feathery leaves when flowering.
Fresh leaves and stems for
seasoning, salsa, soups and
sauces.
Coriander
Round dry seeds formed after cilantro flowers. Seeds turn light
brown when mature and dry.
Dry spice; can be used whole or
ground.
Culantro / Chadon beni
Longer, tougher, saw-toothed leaves in a rosette, stronger
flavor and better heat tolerance. It is a different plant from
cilantro.
Strong Caribbean seasoning herb;
often better for hot lowland
conditions.
 If customers want soft leaves quickly, sell this as cilantro.
 
 If customers let it flower and dry seeds, they can harvest coriander seed.
 
 If customers ask for a heat-tough Caribbean herb, explain that culantro/chadon beni is different from cilantro.

4. Sunlight, Soil and Water
Cilantro grows best as a cool-season leafy herb. In Jamaica-style heat, the main goal is to slow bolting by keeping roots
evenly moist and avoiding harsh afternoon sun.
 Care Area
 Target
Sunlight - cool/mild weather
6+ hours of sun can work well
Sunlight - hot weather
Morning sun + light afternoon shade
Best soil
Loose, rich, well-draining soil
Soil moisture
Evenly moist, not waterlogged
Container soil
Free-draining potting mix with compost
Watering in pots
Check daily in hot weather
Watering in ground
Water during dry spells; do not let plants wilt repeatedly
Mulch
Thin mulch layer to cool soil and reduce drying
Hot, dry conditions speed up flowering. A cooler spot with light shade at midday can give customers a longer leaf
harvest.

5. Feeding Guide
 Stage
 Timing
 What To Do
At planting
Day 0
Mix compost into soil or use a fertile potting mix.
After settling
7-10 days after transplanting
Feed lightly only if leaves are pale or growth is slow.
Leaf harvest stage
Every 2-3 weeks if needed
Use mild liquid feed or compost support. Do not overfeed.
Bolting/flowering stage
When flower stalk rises
Stop pushing leaf growth; harvest leaves or allow seed crop.
 Cilantro is a short-term herb, so it does not need heavy feeding.
 
 Too much nitrogen can make soft growth and may reduce flavor quality.
 
 Feed lightly after the plant settles, not immediately after a stressful transplant.

6. Harvesting Guide
 Harvest Type
 Timing / Method
First leaf harvest
Start when plants are about 6 inches tall.
Cut-and-come-again
Cut outer leaves or stems; do not remove more than 1/3 of the plant at once.
Bunch harvest
Cut plant about 1 inch above soil and allow possible regrowth.
Harvest frequency
Every 3-7 days once plant is established and growing well.
Best harvest time
Early morning after leaves are dry.
Flower harvest
Flowers are edible and attract beneficial insects.
Coriander seed harvest
Collect seeds when they turn light brown and dry, usually 2-3 weeks after flowering.
For leaf customers, advise them to harvest often and replant every 2-3 weeks. For seed customers, allow plants to
flower and dry.

8. Bolting, Flower Stalks and Coriander Seed
Bolting means cilantro is leaving the leafy stage and moving into flowers and seed. In hot weather this can happen quickly.
 Sign
 Meaning
 Action
Plant stretches taller
Flower stalk starting
Harvest leaves immediately.
Leaves become fine/feathery
Bolting has started
Use leaves soon; flavor may become stronger.
Small white/pinkish flowers
Pollinator/seed stage
Leave some plants for bees and coriander seed.
Seeds turn light brown
Coriander seed is ready
Cut stems, dry in a paper bag, store dry.
To slow bolting: use morning sun and afternoon shade, keep soil evenly moist, avoid root disturbance, choose
slow-bolt varieties where available, and plant small batches every 2-3 weeks.

9. Small Farmer Notes
 Practice
 Recommendation
Planting system
Use raised beds or well-drained rows.
Leaf spacing
4-8 inches apart depending on bunch size wanted.
Seed production spacing
8-12 inches apart for stronger seed heads.
Succession planting
Plant a new batch every 2-3 weeks for steady supply.
Heat management
Use afternoon shade or partial shade netting in hot periods.
Harvest handling
Harvest early morning; keep bunches shaded and cool.
Market quality
Sell bright green leaves; remove yellow or bolting stems if selling as leaf cilantro.
Important: for leaf production, cilantro is better managed as a quick, repeated crop instead of one long crop.
Replanting small batches is more reliable than trying to keep one planting leafy for too long.

11. Clean Handling and Seedling Sanitation
Cilantro is a fresh herb that customers may eat raw, so clean handling is important from planting through harvest.
 Sanitation Step
 Why It Matters
Wash hands before handling seedlings or
harvest
Reduces transfer of disease organisms and dirt.
Use clean scissors/knife
Reduces leaf rot and harvest contamination.
Do not harvest diseased leaves for customers
Protects quality and customer confidence.
Do not handle sick plants then healthy plants
Prevents spreading problems across trays or beds.
Keep pets/animals away from herb beds
Reduces contamination risk.
Rinse harvested leaves with clean water
Improves food safety before use.
Tobacco note: cilantro is not the main crop associated with tobacco mosaic virus, but growers should still wash
hands after smoking or handling tobacco before touching any seedlings. This is a good general plant-health practice
across herbs, peppers and tomatoes.

12. Hardening Off Seedlings
If the cilantro came from shade, greenhouse, nursery shelves, or protected trays, harden it off before full outdoor exposure.
 Day
 Exposure
Day 1-2
Bright shade for 2-3 hours. Keep root ball moist.
Day 3-4
Morning sun for 2-4 hours, then shade.
Day 5-6
Morning sun plus longer outdoor time. Avoid harsh midday sun.
Day 7
Transplant early morning or late evening.
 Cilantro has a taproot and dislikes rough transplanting.
 
 Do not let tray cells dry out during hardening off.
 
 If seedlings wilt badly, move them back to shade and water gently.

13. Transplanting from Trays or Small Pots
Cilantro is usually best direct-seeded, but customers may still buy seedlings. The key is to transplant gently and young.
 Step
 Instruction
1
Water seedlings 1-2 hours before transplanting.
2
Prepare holes first so roots are exposed for the shortest time possible.
3
Remove the seedling with the full root ball intact. Do not pull by the stem.
4
Plant at the same depth it was growing in the tray or pot.
5
Space 4-8 inches apart for leaves or 8-12 inches for seed production.
6
Water gently at soil level immediately after planting.
7
Give light shade for 1-3 days if conditions are hot.
8
Do not apply strong fertilizer for the first 7 days after transplanting.
Best time to transplant: before 8:30 a.m. or after 4:30 p.m. In hot weather, late evening is usually safer.

15. Common Diseases
 Disease
 Signs
 Action
Powdery mildew
White powdery coating on leaves
Improve airflow; avoid overcrowding; remove affected
leaves.
Bacterial leaf spot
Dark or water-soaked spots; spotting worse
with wet leaves
Avoid overhead watering; remove affected leaves;
increase spacing.
Damping off
Young seedlings collapse near soil line
Use clean mix; avoid overwatering; improve airflow.
Root rot
Yellowing, wilting, sour/wet soil smell
Improve drainage; reduce watering; avoid standing
water.
General leaf rot
Slimy or rotting leaves near soil
Remove bad leaves; water at soil level; reduce
crowding.
 Water early enough for foliage to dry before night.
 
 Do not crowd seedlings tightly after transplanting.
 
 Remove diseased leaves from the growing area instead of leaving them on the soil.
