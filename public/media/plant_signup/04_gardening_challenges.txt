Gardening asks a lot from the people who love it.

There are changes in weather, temperature, seasons, soil, water, space and location. A very hot summer can test your skill. A new yard or balcony can test your patience. Sometimes the same plant that looked strong yesterday begins struggling today, even when you are doing your best.

That is part of what makes gardeners so resilient.

I remember getting 24 strawberry plants and thinking, at least five of these must survive. One by one, they died. I changed the soil. I moved them. I watered more. I watered less. I tried different methods, and still they faded away. Each plant took a small piece of the dream with it.

The same thing happened with sage seedlings. They came up beautifully, then slowly disappeared no matter what I tried.

Those moments can be heartbreaking, especially when you already imagined the harvest. You think about the fruit, the jam, the meals, the scent of the herbs, the little success you wanted to share with someone. Then reality teaches you something harder.

Still, these losses are not proof that you are not a gardener.

They are part of learning the language of plants, climate, soil and place. Every failed plant teaches us something about timing, care, patience and humility. The challenge is real, but so is the growth that happens in the gardener.
