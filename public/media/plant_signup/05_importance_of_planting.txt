Home gardens matter more than people sometimes realize.

When more homes grow even a few plants, the benefits spread beyond one household. Plants cool the spaces around us. They soften heat, bring life back into yards and verandas, and remind us that every small growing space is part of a larger environment.

Gardening also changes our relationship with food.

So much money passes through markets and shops because most people are not growing even a portion of what they need at home. Farmers then have to stretch themselves to produce huge quantities, often under pressure from weather, cost and demand.

If more families grew herbs, vegetables, fruit trees or even small useful plants at home, the pressure would be shared differently. Food would become more personal. More people would understand what it takes to grow it. More meals would carry something fresh, local and cared for by hand.

Gardening is not only a hobby. Farming is not only a job.

These practices affect the economy, the environment and the health of the human family. When people give up on growing, something bigger than one garden is affected. When people keep planting, they help create a cooler, healthier and more connected world.
